============================================================
0. PURPOSE AND NON-NEGOTIABLE BOUNDARIES
============================================================

This document defines Phase 4 of Allma Safety AI: the Intelligent
Community Responder Network.

The goal is to help Allma identify and safely coordinate suitable,
opted-in community responders near an emergency.

Allma must:

• Find the right person, at the right location, at the right time.
• Match capability, permissions, availability, and safety conditions.
• Use real location and real system events where available.
• Protect the privacy of citizens and responders.
• Prioritize official emergency services for serious incidents.
• Fail safely when data, permissions, routing, or verification is unavailable.

Allma must not:

• Simply search for the nearest user.
• Create a public directory or map of responders.
• Expose exact responder locations.
• Claim a responder, authority, call, or escalation exists when it does not.
• Allow a user to self-declare as police, a doctor, a paramedic, or another
  official authority.
• Encourage ordinary community responders to enter danger zones.
• Replace police, ambulance, fire services, or other official responders.

Community responders are supplementary emergency assistance only.

============================================================
2. CORE PRODUCT EXPERIENCE
============================================================

The intended experience is:

1. A citizen activates SOS.
2. Allma obtains location with explicit permission.
3. Allma assesses the emergency and classifies category, severity, and danger.
4. The secure server searches only eligible, opted-in responders.
5. The system filters by capability, location, freshness, availability,
   permissions, workload, safety policy, and service radius.
6. A small, appropriate group receives a privacy-safe request.
7. A responder accepts only if they can help safely.
8. Allma creates an authorized assignment.
9. The responder receives only the information needed for the assignment.
10. The citizen sees verified status updates from actual system events.
11. The responder can travel, check in, assist, request official help, or stop.
12. The emergency closes, tracking stops, and an audit trail is retained.

The product should feel intelligent, trustworthy, fast, calm, and premium.

============================================================
3. COMMUNITY RESPONDER DEFINITION
============================================================

A Community Responder is an ordinary person who has explicitly opted in to
receive selected emergency assistance requests and has accepted Allma's
safety rules.

Possible responder types:

• Community Volunteer
• First Aid Responder
• Medical Professional — verification required
• Fire Safety Volunteer
• Search & Rescue Volunteer
• Transport Assistance
• Child Safety Volunteer
• Community Leader — verification or administrator approval required
• Other approved emergency helper

Users may not self-assign official titles.

Examples of titles that must never be displayed as confirmed without
appropriate verification:

• Doctor
• Nurse
• Paramedic
• Police
• Fire officer
• Government official
• Authorized emergency responder

If a user selects a professional or authority-related type, display:

    Verification required

Until verification is completed, show a neutral responder label and do not
imply that the person is a licensed professional or official authority.

4. RESPONDER ONBOARDING
============================================================

Provide a dedicated onboarding path separate from ordinary citizen onboarding.

Entry point:

    I want to help my community.

Introductory screen:

    Become an Allma Community Responder

Explain:

    You may receive emergency assistance requests from people near you.
    Only accept requests when you are able and it is safe.

Before activation, the responder must:

• Review the role and its limitations.
• Provide the required profile information.
• Grant location permission explicitly.
• Select the area and categories they can support.
• Accept the responder safety agreement.
• Explicitly opt in to emergency requests.
• Start in Offline status unless activation is deliberate.

Onboarding must be resumable and must not activate a responder silently.

============================================================
6. VERIFICATION LEVELS AND RESPONDER LEVELS
============================================================

6.1 VERIFICATION STATUS

Create the following verification levels:

UNVERIFIED

No identity, skill, organization, or authority verification has been
completed. The user may not be presented as professionally qualified.

BASIC VERIFIED

Identity verification has been completed.

SKILL VERIFIED

A specific emergency skill has been verified. Verification is attached to the
skill, not automatically to every capability.

ORGANIZATION VERIFIED

The responder has been verified through an approved organization.

AUTHORIZED RESPONDER

The responder has been approved by the relevant Allma, government, or partner
authority. This status must never be invented or granted automatically.

6.2 RESPONDER LEVEL

Create the following responder levels:

• Citizen Helper
• Community Responder
• Verified Responder
• Specialist Responder
• Authorized Responder

Each level must have explicit permissions. A higher level must never be
granted automatically because of a profile selection or a single response.

============================================================
7. RESPONDER SAFETY AGREEMENT
============================================================

Before a responder becomes active, show:

    Only respond when it is safe.

The responder must acknowledge:

• Do not confront armed suspects.
• Do not approach active violence.
• Do not enter dangerous buildings.
• Do not chase criminals.
• Do not enter fires.
• Do not provide professional medical treatment unless qualified.
• Do not put yourself or others in danger.
• Stay at a safe distance when official help is required.
• Wait for official emergency services when appropriate.

The responder can decline or stop an assignment without penalty for choosing
personal safety.

One declined request must not automatically punish, deactivate, or downgrade
the responder.

============================================================
8. AVAILABILITY AND CAPACITY
============================================================

Provide a prominent availability control with these states:

🟢 AVAILABLE

The responder may receive eligible emergency requests.

🟡 BUSY

The responder may receive critical requests only, according to system policy.
Do not send new non-critical requests.

🔴 HANDLING EMERGENCY

The responder is actively assigned and must not receive new assignments.

⚫ OFFLINE

The responder must not receive emergency requests.

Also support:

• Unavailable
• Temporary availability
• Availability until a selected time

Temporary availability options:

• Available for 30 minutes
• Available for 1 hour
• Available until a selected time
• Always available

Availability must expire automatically when a temporary window ends.
The user must be able to go offline at any time.

9. SERVICE AREA
============================================================

Allow the responder to select a preferred response radius:

• 500 m
• 1 km
• 2 km
• 5 km
• 10 km
• Custom

The matching engine must not send requests outside the selected area unless

Service-area selection must work with the geographic hierarchy defined in

============================================================
10. LOCATION PRIVACY
============================================================

This is a critical safety requirement.

Never:

• Show responders publicly on a map.
• Expose a responder's exact coordinates to citizens or other responders.
• Allow users to browse everyone nearby.
• Return raw location tables to the client.
• Treat an old location as current.

Before acceptance, show only:

• Emergency category
• Severity
• Approximate distance
• General location area
• Minimal AI-generated summary
• Safety prompt

Example:

    Approximately 650 m away.

Do not show before acceptance:

• Full address
• Exact GPS coordinates
• Private contact details
• Sensitive citizen information
• Unnecessary identity details

After acceptance, disclose only information necessary for the authorized
assignment and only to authorized participants.

============================================================
11. RESPONDER LOCATION
============================================================

Use device location only after the responder has explicitly granted permission.

Store securely:

• Latitude
• Longitude
• Accuracy
• Recorded timestamp
• Availability at the time of recording
• Location freshness
• Current-location marker

Do not continuously track a responder unnecessarily.

Use recent location only when:

• The responder has opted in.
• Location permission is active.
• The responder is eligible.
• The location is within the configured freshness window.

When location becomes stale:

• Mark the location unavailable.
• Remove the responder from ordinary proximity matching.
• Do not present the responder as nearby.
• Do not fabricate a replacement timestamp or distance.

While actively responding, location updates are permitted only according to:

• Responder consent
• Emergency policy
• System configuration
• Authorized participant access

Stop response tracking when:

• The responder completes the assignment.
• The emergency closes.
• The assignment is cancelled.
• The responder withdraws.
• The responder is marked unable to continue.

============================================================
12. SERVER-SIDE MATCHING ENGINE
============================================================

Matching must run on a secure server-side worker, function, or equivalent
trusted backend boundary. The client must never discover responders directly.

Inputs:

• Emergency location
• Emergency category
• Severity
• Immediate-danger flag
• Responder location
• Location freshness
• Responder availability
• Responder skills
• Skill verification
• Responder verification level
• Service radius
• Current assignments
• Emergency permissions
• Response history
• Block and report relationships
• Emergency Circle relationship
• Emergency-specific safety policy

Required eligibility filters:

• Explicit opt-in
• Valid location permission
• Fresh location
• Eligible availability
• Matching service area
• Matching capability or approved general-response capability
• No active block
• No incompatible current assignment
• Appropriate verification for the incident
• No policy restriction for the incident category

The server must fail closed when required information is missing.

============================================================
13. MATCHING SCORE
============================================================

Use a configurable internal score. Example weights:

• Distance: 35%
• Emergency skill match: 25%
• Availability: 15%
• Verification: 10%
• Emergency-category compatibility: 10%
• Current workload: 5%

These values must be configuration, not hardcoded business logic.

Additional policy adjustments may account for:

• Emergency Circle relationship
• Immediate danger
• Responder safety authorization
• Response history
• Current workload
• Area-specific policies

Never expose the raw score to ordinary citizens or responders.

Administrators may see a controlled explanation such as:

    Selected because:
    ✓ Approximately 650 m away
    ✓ First Aid skill
    ✓ Available
    ✓ Skill verified
    ✓ Suitable for a medical emergency

Do not expose security-sensitive scoring details.

============================================================
14. REAL GEOGRAPHIC DISTANCE
============================================================

Calculate distance from real latitude and longitude.

Do not use:

• Random numbers
• Placeholder distances presented as real
• Stale coordinates treated as current
• Client-only distance calculations for authorization

Use geospatial calculations and, where supported, PostGIS or equivalent
geospatial indexes.

Example internal ordering:

• Responder A — 420 m
• Responder B — 1.1 km
• Responder C — 2.7 km

Return only an approximate, privacy-safe distance to the responder.

============================================================
15. LOCATION FRESHNESS
============================================================

Use clear freshness states:

CURRENT

Excellent. Updated seconds ago.

RECENT

Good. Updated within the recent operating window.

STALE

Poor. The location is too old for ordinary matching.

UNAVAILABLE

No recent usable location.

Example display:

• Excellent — Updated 8 seconds ago
• Good — Updated 2 minutes ago
• Poor — Updated 20 minutes ago
• Unavailable — No recent location

Freshness thresholds must be configurable. Never use stale location as if it
were current and never claim certainty that the data does not support.

============================================================
16. EMERGENCY COMPATIBILITY
============================================================

Medical emergency:

Prioritize:

• First Aid Responder
• Verified medical skill
• Appropriate health responder
• General community responder when safe

Crime, active violence, or dangerous incident:

Prioritize:

• Official emergency pathway
• Authorized or specifically trained responders
• Trusted contacts where appropriate

Do not send ordinary responders toward active violent crime, armed attacks,
fires, or dangerous crime.

Fire:

• Prioritize official fire services.
• Do not encourage ordinary responders to enter a fire or dangerous building.
• Use only specifically trained and authorized responders when policy permits.

Road accident:

• Consider a verified first aider.
• Consider a traffic or community safety responder.
• Avoid unnecessary crowding.

Missing or vulnerable person:

• Consider approved community responders.
• Consider trusted contacts.
• Notify relevant authorities when appropriate.

Official emergency response remains the priority for serious incidents.

============================================================
17. CRITICAL AND NON-CRITICAL EMERGENCIES
============================================================

17.1 CRITICAL EMERGENCIES

For critical emergencies, the system may notify multiple appropriate
responders without unnecessarily waiting for one response.

Example:

• Nearby first aider
• Trusted emergency contact
• Official medical emergency pathway

Use configured notification waves and avoid unnecessary crowding.

17.2 NON-CRITICAL EMERGENCIES

For lower-severity emergencies, notify fewer responders.

Example — lost elderly person:

• Nearby approved community responders
• Trusted contacts
• Relevant authorities when appropriate

Severity and immediate-danger rules must be evaluated server-side.

============================================================
19. ACCEPT FLOW
============================================================

When a responder taps ACCEPT:

1. Revalidate eligibility on the server.
2. Confirm the request is still available.
3. Confirm the responder can safely accept.
4. Create a Responder Assignment atomically.
5. Set assignment status to ACCEPTED.
6. Update responder capacity to Handling Emergency where appropriate.
7. Share only the authorized emergency details.
8. Emit an authorized responder-accepted event.
9. Notify the citizen using the actual event.

Citizen message:

    [Responder name] has accepted your emergency request.

The responder may then receive:

• Relevant emergency details
• Authorized map or emergency area
• Navigation
• In-app call
• Emergency chat
• Verified emergency status

Do not expose personal phone numbers.

Acceptance must be idempotent. A duplicate tap must not create duplicate
assignments.

============================================================
20. DECLINE AND NOT-NOW FLOW
============================================================

Decline reasons are optional:

• Unavailable
• Too far
• Unsafe
• Not my skill
• Other

Do not require a reason.

After decline:

• Mark the notification declined.
• Preserve the reason only when supplied.
• Do not punish the responder automatically.
• Do not notify the same responder again for the same emergency.
• Continue to the next eligible responder or wave when appropriate.

NOT NOW should dismiss or defer the request according to configured policy
without implying acceptance.

============================================================
21. MULTIPLE RESPONDERS AND FLOOD CONTROL
============================================================

Multiple responders may be useful for some incidents, such as:

• Road accident:
  First Aid Responder + traffic/community responder
• Critical medical emergency:
  First Aid Responder + trusted contact + official pathway

Do not create unnecessary crowds or duplicate assignments.

Use staged notification waves:

Example:

• First wave: 3 appropriate responders
• If nobody accepts: next wave of 3
• Critical emergencies: larger configured wave when justified

Every emergency must have a configured maximum notification and assignment
capacity.

    Are you able to respond safely?

• Yes, I'm safe to respond

• Return them to the responder workspace.
• Consider another eligible responder.

For dangerous incidents, ordinary responders must see:

    authorized emergency services.

Specifically trained and authorized responders may be handled by a separate

============================================================
24. RESPONDER CHECK-IN AND ARRIVAL
============================================================

• I've Arrived
• Unable To Reach

• Escalate the emergency through the existing official pathway.
• Notify authorized participants.
• Record the event in the audit trail.

GPS proximity may support a suggestion, but it must not automatically claim
that the responder arrived.

The responder must explicitly select:

    I've Arrived

• Assistance Complete
• Official Help Arrived
• No Assistance Needed
• Unable To Assist
• Unsafe Situation

• Stop responder tracking.
• Stop unnecessary location updates.
• Stop new assignment notifications for the closed event.
• Persist the final status.
• Create an audit record.
• Preserve authorized history without exposing sensitive citizen data.

============================================================
42. RELEASE ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
============================================================

The phase is not ready for production emergency traffic until:

• Matching is server-side and authorization-protected.
• Only explicitly opted-in responders can be considered.
• Exact responder coordinates are not publicly exposed.
• Stale locations are excluded from ordinary matching.
• Professional and authority claims require verification.
• Dangerous incidents do not direct ordinary responders into danger.
• Official response pathways remain prioritized.
• Notification and assignment transitions are idempotent.
• Duplicate emergency contacts are prevented.
• Realtime events are participant-scoped.
• Demo mode is visibly separate from real emergency state.
• Every important action is auditable.
• The Phase 1, Phase 2, and Phase 3 flows continue to work.

============================================================
43. CRITICAL FINAL RULE
============================================================

Allma Community Responders are supplementary emergency assistance.

They do not replace:

• Police
• Ambulance
• Fire services
• Other official emergency services

For serious emergencies, Allma must prioritize official emergency response
pathways.

Community responders must never be instructed to put themselves or others in
danger.
